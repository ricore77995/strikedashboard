# Adversarial Critique — StrikeLab v3.2 (candidate-A)

WEAKNESS-1 [FATAL]: §4.3 "Prize economics — break-even shown" — The break-even table is internally incoherent at the gym's actual scale. The spec assumes "top quartile ≈ 38 students" reaches at least one threshold, but 38 is roughly 25% of the entire 150-subscriber base — and the table then claims ~25 Bronze winners, ~8 Silver, ~3 Gold, ~1 Diamond totaling 37 winners/month. That means a quarter of the entire subscriber base wins a prize EVERY month, in perpetuity. For a gym with churn, ~150 subs, and a Bronze threshold of 2,500 pts (reachable by any disciplined P12 with stacked weekend boosts — see §4.2's "realistic median P12 = 1,070" PLUS boosts not modeled), Bronze is functionally a participation prize. The €535/mo figure is therefore a floor, not a steady state: as students learn the system, the proportion crossing 2,500 will trend toward 50%+, doubling cost. The "6% of MRR" claim that anchors the whole economic argument is built on an unmodeled assumption that point distribution stays static under behavioral adaptation.

WEAKNESS-2 [MAJOR]: §2.5 step 5 vs §2.5 final paragraph — Internal contradiction on the meaning of the snapshot. Step 5 says "Zero monthly_points in batches of 50" AFTER the seal in step 3, but the closing paragraph states "the snapshot is informational once sealed. This eliminates the entire class of 'streak broke because timezone was wrong' bugs." Streaks are tracked in `gamification_state`, not the snapshot — and `monthly_points` zeroing has nothing to do with streak attribution. The paragraph conflates monthly point sealing with streak boundary semantics, but §5.3 never says where the streak counter lives, when it zeroes, or how it interacts with the cron. The "eliminates the entire class" claim is rhetorical, not demonstrated.

WEAKNESS-3 [MAJOR]: §2.5 advisory lock — The recovery path is unsafe. "If a `running` row exists < 30 min old, abort (concurrent run); > 30 min, log alarm and proceed (recovery)." Vercel cron jobs can stall for many reasons (cold start, network, Turso failover). After 30 minutes the spec ASSUMES the prior run died, but it may merely be slow — at which point two writers race against `monthly_points_snapshot` (a table the spec calls "never mutated"). The lock provides no fencing token, no atomic CAS on the meta row, and no way to detect partial completion (e.g., seal succeeded, batches 1–3 done, batch 4 hangs). "Retries skip customers already zeroed" depends on the prior writer having committed — which the lock cannot prove.

WEAKNESS-4 [MAJOR]: §4.2 stacking cap math — The spec claims "stacked legitimate boosts (weekend ×1.8 on 2 classes, streak_10 ×1.6, renovação ×1.5 — capped at ×3.0) a maxed P8 in a renewal month lands 4,500–6,000 pts." But §6 says "delta-sum formula" with a ×3.0 cap. Delta-sum means (1.8 - 1) + (1.6 - 1) + (1.5 - 1) = 0.8 + 0.6 + 0.5 = 1.9 → effective multiplier 2.9 (under cap). Applied to a P8 base of 2,680 + bonuses, the result is ~7,700, not 4,500–6,000. So either (a) the cap is being misapplied in the worked example, (b) the formula is multiplicative-with-cap, or (c) the worked example is wrong. The spec's pride point — "every formula has a concrete walk-through" — is undermined by the very example provided.

WEAKNESS-5 [MAJOR]: §3.2 erasure step 1 vs §2.2 identity resolution — Erasure tombstones `gamification_identity` and "PII nulled, customer_id retained as opaque hash." But §2.2 says `customer_id` IS the Yogo customer_id, which is itself PII (it identifies a living individual in Yogo). Replacing the row's *other fields* with hashes doesn't anonymize anything as long as the same `customer_id` appears in `gamification_event_log`, `monthly_points_snapshot`, AND the live Yogo system — anyone with Yogo access trivially re-identifies. The "opaque hash" framing is GDPR theatre; under WP29 / EDPB guidance this is pseudonymization, not anonymization, and the retained event log payloads are still personal data subject to the very erasure right being claimed. The 5-year retention in step 7 for `erasure_applied` audits is also unjustified — "legitimate interest, legal defense" is asserted without a balancing test.

WEAKNESS-6 [MAJOR]: §7 weekly challenges with N=150 — At 150 subscribers, opt-in rate ≥40% target = 60 participants. A "Flash Check-in" with `winners_max` (number unspecified — the spec never gives N) split across one weekly challenge means winners are 3–5 people. Over a year that's ~150–250 challenge wins distributed across maybe 30–40 distinct active engagers. The selection algorithm is elaborate (atomic ordered SELECT, idempotency keys, race-free against Yogo time) but the underlying scale doesn't justify the engineering. Meanwhile `winners_max` itself is never numerically specified — the implementer has to pick.

WEAKNESS-7 [MAJOR]: §12 capacity estimate — "MVP = Phase 0 + Phase 1 = 8 weeks of calendar time… 15h/week of code time." Phase 1 alone lists: monthly+lifetime credit, plan-aware values, tier evaluation, Art. 22 queue, 8+ triggers, 4 boosts, monthly reset cron with sealing, snapshot table, prize tiers, POS redemption, inventory, prize-cap toggle, CSV/API discount tool, Diamante review queue, late-credit queue, comeback wall. That's ~16 distinct deliverables in 60 hours = 3.75h per deliverable including design, code, test, deploy, docs. For a system that also requires a passing §13.1 replay test, idempotency test, month-boundary test, race test, and Marcelo training — this is the same "naively optimistic" mistake the spec accuses v3.1 of, just with 8 weeks instead of 6.

WEAKNESS-8 [MAJOR]: §2.3 "All temporal triggers reference Yogo-side timestamps" — Hidden assumption never demonstrated. The spec uses `yogo.check_in_recorded_at` in §7 and `last_renewed_at` in §2.3, but never establishes that the Yogo API actually exposes these fields with sufficient resolution and stability. The yogo-booking-api skill notes explicitly that some Yogo endpoints have undocumented semantics. If `check_in_recorded_at` is actually the booking time, or rounded to the class start, the "race-free under polling lag" claim in §7 collapses — multiple students checking in to the same class would have identical timestamps, defeating the "ORDER BY Yogo timestamp ASC" tie-break for Flash Check-in.

WEAKNESS-9 [MAJOR]: §10.1 "lawful basis becomes contract" for `/postei` — Legal reasoning is wrong. The student sending a link to their IG post is voluntary, but processing that link, fetching oEmbed metadata, storing it, awarding points based on it, and cross-referencing with check-ins is not "performance of a contract" — the gym's subscription contract does not require gamification. Article 6(1)(b) "contract" basis applies to processing necessary to perform the contract; gamification is, by the spec's own §1 principle 1, opt-in. The correct basis is consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) or possibly legitimate interest, and the spec's flip from "consent" (v3.1) to "contract" (v3.2) is a regression that increases legal risk while the DPIA is being claimed as proportionate.

WEAKNESS-10 [MAJOR]: §9.1 deferred re-entry — "A student who would re-enter Champions League by winning their category in N+1 while in Champions League is deferred one month — they return to regular ranking in N+2 and enter Champions League in N+3." This punishes the most engaged students by removing them from the regular monthly prize pool in N+1 (per the same section) AND from Champions League in N+2. A student who dominates two consecutive months is locked out of all rewards for one month. For a 150-person community where 3–5 students likely drive most engagement, this directly demotivates the top of the funnel. Contradicts §1 principle 2 "reward training first."

WEAKNESS-11 [MINOR]: §2.1 generated columns — `lisbon_local_date` as a generated DATE "from `created_at` at insert time using `AT TIME ZONE 'Europe/Lisbon'`". Turso/libSQL generated columns are SQLite-flavored; SQLite does not natively support `AT TIME ZONE` — that's PostgreSQL syntax. The spec asserts a feature the chosen DB doesn't have. The implementer is silently handed a portability landmine.

WEAKNESS-12 [MINOR]: §15 fallback ~50 LOC — "~50 LOC, off-peak detection lag up to 30 min — invisible to students because no streak/challenge timer references off-peak hours." But §7 weekly challenges run Wed 12:00 → Sun 23:59, which straddles every defined off-peak window the appendix lists. "Flash Check-in" timing precision matters most at challenge start (Wed 12:00) and end (Sun 23:59) — both off-peak hours under the table. The "invisible to students" claim is false for exactly the feature that needs the tightest polling.

WEAKNESS-13 [MINOR]: §13.2 "DPIA signed off by Ricardo and one external reviewer (lawyer or experienced DPO)" — sourcing and paying this reviewer is unscheduled. §10.3 says fractional DPO is only contracted at >250 subscribers OR regulator inquiry, but launch criteria require one to sign off the DPIA. So the spec simultaneously says "no DPO contracted" AND "DPIA must be signed by a DPO before launch." That's a launch blocker hidden in plain sight.

VERDICT: The economic break-even in §4.3 is built on assuming static behavior at small N — once 40% opt-in is hit, Bronze becomes a participation prize for a quarter of all subscribers monthly, and the "6% of MRR" anchor that justifies shipping at all is the first thing to break under success.
